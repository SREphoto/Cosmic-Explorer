# Cosmic Explorer – Unified Game Systems Plan
## Shining-in-the-Darkness Menu + Sim Planet + How They Play as One Game

**Status:** Active design plan  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-25  
**Based on locked decisions from player direction**

---

## 1. High-Level Vision

Cosmic Explorer has two intertwined layers that feel like one continuous experience:

| Layer | What it is | Player feeling |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| **Action Layer** | 2D planet-orbiting jumper runs | Skill, risk, short exciting sessions |
| **Home Layer** | Planet sphere + side-scrolling town & buildings | Belonging, story, management, long-term progress |

The Home Layer is where the player lives.  
The Action Layer is where the player goes out to gather resources, complete challenges, and return home stronger.

Everything the player does on a run feeds the home planet.  
Everything the player does on the home planet makes future runs more interesting or powerful.

---

## 2. The Home Layer – Two Nested Views

### A. Planet Sphere (Home Screen)

- After login / returning from a run, the player sees their **personal planet**.
- The planet fills most of the screen and can be freely rotated (drag = spin the sphere).
- Key locations are **physically attached** to the surface and move with the planet:
  - Town
  - Farms / Gardens
  - Hangar
  - Future districts (as the sim expands)
- Player taps a location → cinematic transition begins.

**Transition (locked):**
1. Camera zooms toward the chosen location on the sphere
2. Screen fades to black
3. Location-appropriate music starts
4. Darkness fades up into the side-scrolling POV scene

### B. Side-Scrolling POV Scenes (Shining in the Darkness style)

Every location the player enters is a **wide scene**:

- Wider than the screen
- Player character is **never visible** (pure first-person / POV)
- Player pans left/right (and limited up/down) to explore
- NPCs, counters, doors, interactables, and story beats are placed along the width
- Scenes can later be extended left or right without redesigning existing content

**Core locations (starting set):**
- Town Main Street (the hub)
- Hangar
- Parts Store / Shop
- Gym / Upgrades
- Greenhouse / Gardens
- Bank
- Warehouse
- Trophy / Medals Hall
- Habitat / Living Quarters
- Workshop

Town Street is the social and story heart.  
Individual buildings are where specific systems live.

## 3. The Sim Planet Layer

The planet is not just a menu — it is a living management sandbox that grows over time.

### Core Loop
1. Go on jumper runs → earn resources, discover items, complete objectives
2. Return to planet → invest resources into buildings, farms, upgrades, and story tasks
3. Planet improves → new capabilities, better rewards, new story beats unlock
4. Repeat

### Progressive Systems (in priority order we already set)
1. **Home Screen + Naming** – planet identity, unique town name
2. **Town + Story** – NPCs, tasks, narrative drive
3. **Monetization** – time + resource costs, speed-ups, fair free path
4. **Sim Planet systems** – districts, habitability, events
5. **Farming & Trading** – deep production and economy (NPC trading first)

### What “managing the planet” feels like
- Looking at the sphere and seeing your town and farms grow
- Entering the Greenhouse to plant and harvest
- Upgrading buildings that visually change both the interior and the planet surface
- Completing story tasks that open new areas or systems
- Watching the planet become more alive (lights, greenery, activity)

---

## 4. How the Two Layers Feel Like One Game

### Shared Identity
- The same art language and charm (cute, readable, Shining-inspired + cosmic frontier)
- The same gesture language (drag planet, pan scenes, tap to enter)
- The same resources and progression currency
- The same NPCs and story threads

### Clear Rhythm
- **Short sessions** → jump into a run from the Hangar, play, return
- **Longer sessions** → stay on the planet, talk to NPCs, manage farms, upgrade, advance story
- The planet is always waiting and always improving

### Narrative Glue
- Town NPCs give tasks that send the player on runs
- Runs uncover story fragments, rare resources, or visitors
- Returning home advances relationships and unlocks new planet features
- The town feels like a living place the player is building a life in

### Visual Continuity
- Planet surface shows the consequences of management (more lights, bigger farms, upgraded hangar)
- Entering a location feels like descending into a real place, not opening a menu
- Leaving a location returns the player to the same rotating planet they left

## 5. Player Journey (First Hours)

1. **Login / Title** – strong header art
2. **Name the town** (unique across server)
3. **Arrive on the Planet Sphere** – first view of home
4. **Enter Town** – meet first NPCs, receive first tasks
5. **Visit Hangar** – understand how to launch a run
6. **First run** – gather resources, feel the action loop
7. **Return** – spend resources, plant something, talk to someone, see the first small change on the planet
8. **Repeat** with growing systems and story

---

## 6. Technical & Design Pillars (for cohesion)

- **One camera language**: sphere rotation → zoom transition → wide POV pan
- **One art direction**: charming, readable, cozy-cosmic (Shining cuteness + existing Cosmic Explorer warmth)
- **One economy**: resources earned in runs are spent on the planet; planet upgrades improve runs
- **One story spine**: NPCs and tasks live in the town and reach into both layers
- **Expandable scenes**: every wide POV location can grow later without breaking old content
- **Future-ready**: data and UI containers leave room for Phase 2 (visiting, trading, light social)

---

## 7. What We Are Explicitly Not Doing Yet

- Player-to-player visiting / trading / combat (Phase 2)
- Full 3D on-planet walking
- Complex multi-colonist AI
- Heavy factory automation

These stay parked so the core fantasy stays focused and shippable.

---

## 8. Success Feeling

When the systems work together, the player should feel:

> “This is my planet.  
> I go out, I come home, I make it better,  
> the town remembers me,  
> and every run means something.”

That is the single game we are building.
